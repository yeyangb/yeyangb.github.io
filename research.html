Research

Understanding extremes
in a changing climate.

How do human influence, atmospheric circulation, and internal variability shape extreme weather events?

01

Extreme-event attribution

Quantifying how anthropogenic forcing changes the likelihood and intensity of extreme events, and connecting probabilistic attribution with circulation-constrained storyline perspectives.

02

Compound & clustered extremes

Understanding how heat and heavy precipitation combine across regions, and how repeated or simultaneous heatwaves can accumulate impacts across space and time.

03

Atmospheric circulation

Examining circulation anomalies, teleconnections, wave activity, and land–atmosphere interactions that organize regional extremes and modulate their response to warming.

04

Machine learning for attribution

Exploring models conditioned on circulation and climate background to connect the behavior of individual events with changes in overall event probability.

Research questions

  • How does warming change the intensity and spatial extent of hemispheric heatwave clusters?
  • What links heat and heavy precipitation in neighboring regions?
  • How can conditional machine-learning models inform estimates of overall event risk?

Methods & data

My doctoral research combined storyline and probability-based attribution, including flow analogues and constructed flow analogues. I worked with observations, ERA5 reanalysis, HadGEM3-A-N216 atmospheric model experiments, and CMIP6 coupled model simulations.
